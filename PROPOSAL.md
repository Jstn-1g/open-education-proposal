# A proposal for education with useful technology

Technology should help people learn, create, question, and work together. It should also make it easier to see what a tool contributed and what a learner understands.

We propose an evidence-reporting practice for learning resources and educational technology. Version 0.1.0 is a draft for criticism, not an adopted standard or a proven intervention.

## The change we want

When an activity claims to show independent understanding, report instructional assistance separately from evidence on fresh work. Describe the task, relevant access support, and limits of the inference.

**Instructional help** teaches, hints at, or supplies the skill being assessed. **Access support** removes a barrier without supplying that skill. Whether a support changes the task depends on what is being assessed; a tool cannot settle that distinction by label alone.

For example, reading a mathematics prompt aloud may preserve the intended mathematics task. Reading the target words aloud would change a task assessing word decoding. Accessibility and subject specialists should review ambiguous cases together.

## What that could look like

Consider a fictional example for adult discussion, not a ready-to-use lesson:

| Observation | What it supports |
| --- | --- |
| A worked fraction-strip example helps a learner compare two fractions referring to the same whole. | An assisted response on that task. |
| Later, the learner explains a fresh comparison without a teaching hint, with appropriate access support. | A separate observation that still needs a valid task and careful interpretation. |

One fresh answer does not establish mastery, retention, transfer, or an effective learning product. Those claims require suitable evidence. Our [source notes](docs/SOURCES.md) distinguish research findings from design choices.

## Five commitments to challenge and improve

1. **Keep people in charge.** Human teaching, relationships, play, collaboration, and judgment remain central. We do not measure a child's worth, curiosity, personality, or compliance.
2. **Keep help and access available.** Independence does not mean isolation. Learning with others and using tools can be worthwhile outcomes; describe the support when interpreting the work.
3. **Make the essentials inspectable.** The ambition is openly licensed content, explanations, rules, tests, and limitations that work without a proprietary model.
4. **Let evidence shape the method.** Compare a proposed tool with a credible simpler approach. AI must earn a role; non-AI, paper, and no-build outcomes remain valid choices.
5. **Build within clear limits.** This community phase is intended for adult proposal and website work. It does not recruit children, collect learner records, run child studies, or build robot tutors or simulated companions.

## What the community can decide first

Make the proposal understandable. Test its distinctions with fictional examples. Correct its sources. Improve access to the public material. Record disagreements and reasons before expanding the scope.

A future learning resource, child participation, or research programme would need its own design, review, and accountable decision. Joining this proposal does not enroll anyone in those activities.
