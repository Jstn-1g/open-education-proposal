# The first 30 days after launch

Version 0.1.0 starts with an adult proposal, evidence notes, an informational website, and a contributor kit. The immediate aim is a clearer proposal and three small pieces of reviewed work—not a complete curriculum or a learning app.

The dates below are **planning targets, not predictions or promises**, measured from the repository's opening for contributions. They do not claim that the work or a hosted website has been completed.

Jstn-1g is the only confirmed maintainer. Work on a small reviewable piece at a time, and confirm a willing reviewer before expanding work that needs specialist judgment. A contributor can begin with one case, one paragraph, or one barrier report. If review capacity is missing, keep the draft and its unanswered questions visible; defer additional work rather than imply that review happened.

## Days 1–7: make the work easy to understand

Keep the [three first tasks](FIRST-CONTRIBUTIONS.md) visible and bounded. Jstn-1g handles the initial response and identifies the expertise needed for review. Explain what a useful contribution looks like and how someone can help without coding.

Invite willing adult readers to answer any of these questions in their own words:

- What is this project trying to change?
- What does the first release offer, and what does it not do?
- What is one useful thing you could contribute next?

Use misunderstandings to improve the wording, not to score readers. This is optional community feedback, not formal participant research or a validated usability result. No personal background, health details, learner records, or stories about identifiable children are needed. Do not request classroom trials or endorsement of the whole project.

## Days 8–21: aim for three inspectable outputs

| Output | What makes it useful | Human review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Six fictional help-versus-access cases | Each names the target skill, support, classification, and uncertainty; includes read-aloud in mathematics and decoding; links two relevant sources with limits | An educator and accessibility practitioner review the distinctions; one person may cover both areas if qualified. Unresolved cases remain labeled. |
| One evidence note | Checks the Bastani paper's claim, citation, methods, population, short-term outcome, and limits; proposes a correction or a reason to retain our wording | A source-literate adult checks the primary paper and the note. Broader educational claims need relevant expertise. |
| One accessibility report and maintainer response | Describes a barrier; records a remedy, a question, or a reason to defer. If a fix is made, recheck it in the same conditions. Unresolved barriers remain visible | A person reviews the reading or interaction task. Reporting requires no fix or proof of disability. Automated checks do not establish whole-site conformance. |

Use the detailed acceptance criteria in the task briefs. If no external contributor takes a task, the maintainer may prepare a draft and request review. A maintainer or AI-generated draft is not evidence of community uptake.

## Days 22–30: review, correct, and choose the next step

Jstn-1g remains accountable for merge and scope decisions. GPT-6 may assist drafting, source comparison, coding, and checks; it does not supply independent educator judgment or become the responsible maintainer. Record reviewers, relevant expertise, material disagreements, and reasons for decisions. State when work has only received self-review.

At day 30, publish a short review of the three outputs and choose one direction:

- **Continue narrowly** if an external educator has examined a task or supplied a concrete counterexample, the work is becoming clearer, and the maintainer can handle review and reports. This is an early participation signal, not proof of learning effectiveness or widespread demand.
- **Narrow or revise** if no external educator engages after a clear invitation and reasonable opportunity to respond. Check whether the task is useful and the invitation reached appropriate people; revise the question or reduce the ask before adding features. Silence alone does not disprove the educational idea.
- **Reduce intake** if review or private-report handling exceeds available capacity. Finish or clearly defer existing work before soliciting more.

## What progress would mean

Useful signals are a corrected claim, a clearer example, a reader who can explain the project and a next step, a resolved access barrier, a reasoned disagreement, and a contributor whose work receives a human response. A few clear explanations do not prove that the project is widely understood. Stars, generated pages, code volume, and AI output are not substitutes.

## Later: earn the next step

The next milestone is a better adult-reviewed proposal. Only then consider a small, openly licensed learning resource with a clear learning purpose, willing qualified reviewers, accessible alternatives, maintenance capacity, and evidence appropriate to its claims. A complete learning path is a longer-term ambition; we have not established the people or evidence needed to deliver it. Technology remains optional and inspectable.

This month does not include child-facing activities, participant research, learner data, robot tutors, or AI companions. A resource or evaluation beyond this scope requires its own plan and accountable decision; this roadmap does not authorize one.
