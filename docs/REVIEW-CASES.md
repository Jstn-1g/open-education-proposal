# Six fictional cases: help, access, and what we can conclude

**Adult discussion draft · revision 0.1 · 9 September 2026**

**Not yet specialist-reviewed.** Prepared with GPT-6 assistance for Jstn-1g's open education proposal. This is a starting draft for [task #1](https://github.com/Jstn-1g/open-education-proposal/issues/1), not a completed community contribution or evidence of adoption.

Use this to question our reasoning with other adults. It is not a lesson, assessment, accommodation decision, or instruction to test children. Every situation below is invented. Do not add learner names, records, diagnoses, scores, or identifiable stories.

## Start with one question

Read one case. Does the claimed skill match what the support leaves for the learner to do? Name one uncertainty or suggest a narrower claim. You do not have to fill in the whole table, classify a person, or find a citation before asking a question.

**Keep necessary support available.** Where support and the target skill overlap, describe supported performance, narrow the claim, or leave it unresolved. Never withdraw necessary support to manufacture an independent result. This proposal does not require logging every hint or testing every activity. Help-seeking, collaboration, and tool use are not failures.

## The cases

The interpretations are our provisional design judgments, not findings established by the sources or rules for deciding anyone's access.

| ID | Intended skill or outcome | Fictional support | Provisional interpretation | What needs scrutiny |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Explain which of two fractions is greater. Decoding written words is not the target. | A prompt is read aloud without explaining the comparison or emphasizing an answer. | Access support may preserve the mathematical task. The response still needs its own interpretation. | Could wording, emphasis, or a description of a diagram supply the comparison strategy? Subject and access reviewers must examine the exact task. |
| C2 | Decode an unfamiliar printed word. | The target word is spoken aloud before the response. | The support supplies the pronunciation being examined. Describe supported word work, not independent print decoding. | The same read-aloud support has a different role than in C1. Keep necessary support; do not turn this example into an instruction to remove it. |
| C3 | Explain a scientific idea in the learner's own words; spelling and handwriting are not targets. | Speech-to-text transcribes a dictated explanation without generating or rewriting its ideas. | This may remove an expression barrier rather than supply scientific understanding. | Recognition errors or automatic rewriting can change the explanation. What did the tool actually do, and can the intended expression be preserved? |
| C4 | Choose and justify a mathematical model for a practical problem. | A calculator or spreadsheet computes from entered values; some configurations also select formulas. | Mixed or unresolved until the tool's role is known. Computing a result and choosing the model are different contributions. | Does this particular setup supply the model or only arithmetic? Keep necessary tools and narrow the claim; no tool-wide allow/ban label settles this. |
| C5 | Explain why the steps of a solution work. | A worked example or AI explanation provides the steps and reasoning, which the learner repeats. | Instructional help supported the response. It does not alone establish independent explanation. | Could the wording imply that using help is cheating or failure? Later evidence, if needed, requires valid work and proportionate interpretation, not compulsory retesting. |
| C6 | Develop a reasoned plan collaboratively, using people and tools well. | Peers propose ideas; an accessible shared document helps the group revise them. | Supported, collaborative performance is itself the intended outcome. There is no need to recast it as isolated individual performance. | A group result cannot establish each person's independent mastery. What claim is actually useful, without ranking people or recording every interaction? |

## What would change our minds?

- A target skill is poorly specified, or a supposedly neutral support supplies part of it.
- The distinction pressures people to refuse help, hides a valid collaborative outcome, or increases unnecessary recording.
- A case creates more teacher interpretation work than the decision warrants. Say which decision it clarifies, or why it should be removed.
- A subject or accessibility practitioner identifies an exclusion, inaccurate assumption, or better explanation. Label unresolved disagreement instead of forcing agreement.

## Sources and their limits

1. [National Center on Educational Outcomes: Accessibility & Accommodations for General Assessments](https://nceo.info/Assessments/general_assessment/accessibility-and-accommodations), accessed 9 September 2026. Its guidance distinguishes changes that preserve what an assessment measures from changes that alter it, and emphasizes individual needs. It concerns general assessments, including policy-specific decisions; it does not validate these six fictional cases. Our draft must not replace local assessment or accommodation decisions.
2. [CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0, consideration 4.2](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/interaction/assistive-technologies/), CAST (2024), accessed 9 September 2026. This guidance supports accessible tools and the support needed to use them, while retaining the learning goal's challenge. It is a design framework, not evidence that our classifications or reporting proposal improve learning.
