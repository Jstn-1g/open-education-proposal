# Design for people, not just performance

Our ambition is open learning that helps people understand, create, question, and participate in their communities. A score is not the purpose of education. Useful technology must support human teaching and leave room for curiosity, relationships, play, and rest.

**Status: proposed design choices for adult review.** This brief is not a validated learning model, a clinical framework, or permission to work with children. No learning resource or child-facing system is being approved here. The [source notes](SOURCES.md) separate research, guidance, and our interpretation; neither this list nor AI review covers every psychological risk.

## Questions a future resource must answer

These are review prompts, not a checklist that certifies safety. An author can begin with one question or fictional counterexample.

| Area | Proposed design choice | Question that could change the design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose and development | Name a specific learning purpose, age range, prerequisites, language, and setting. Include knowledge, reasoning, creative or collaborative work as appropriate; do not reduce a broad subject to easily counted answers. | Who is this unsuitable for? Does evidence from a different age or setting actually apply? |
| Motivation and agency | Offer meaningful choices alongside clear goals, explanations, examples, and help. Allow pauses and mistakes without shame. Avoid coercive streaks, public comparison, or rewards tied to staying online. | Does the choice matter? Can someone ask for help or stop without losing access or being labeled unmotivated? |
| Belonging and relationships | Keep teachers and peers central. Welcome disagreement and different ways of participating. Do not substitute mindset slogans or simulated affection for material support. | Whose language, culture, or experience is missing? Could an example humiliate, stereotype, or pressure someone to disclose personal experiences? |
| Cognitive support | Match challenge to the task and prior knowledge. Consider worked examples, spaced revisiting, retrieval, explanation, and useful feedback; no single technique is a universal recipe. | Is difficulty helping learning, or merely overloading the learner? Is the content accurate, and can misconceptions be corrected? |
| Access and fairness | Keep necessary access support available. Provide usable non-AI and low-bandwidth or printable alternatives where the task permits. Review language, sensory, motor, and other access barriers without demanding diagnoses. | Does the alternative preserve the learning purpose? Is the tool doing the target skill, or removing an unrelated barrier? |
| Evidence without surveillance | Use a proportionate observation only when a claim needs it. Describe assistance and uncertainty; do not turn an activity result into a permanent ability label, ranking, or high-stakes decision. | What decision would this evidence change? Could we answer with less recording, or no learner record at all? |
| Well-being and privacy | Protect room for movement, play, rest, and trusted human support. Do not infer emotions, mental health, personality, or honesty from clicks, voice, face, speed, or errors. No therapeutic claims or AI companions. | What human interaction or activity would this displace? Can a concern be raised without public disclosure or automated psychological judgment? |
| Workload and feasibility | Count preparation, adaptation, checking, reporting, maintenance, device, connectivity, and family costs. Compare with a credible simpler approach. Name a responsible human and an available reviewer. | What work is removed, and what new work is created? Can the people involved actually sustain it? |

These choices draw on the [learning evidence](SOURCES.md#learning-and-evidence), [development and motivation notes](SOURCES.md#development-motivation-and-feasibility), and [rights and access guidance](SOURCES.md#rights-access-and-openness). They are not effects established for this project. Engagement is not the same as learning; a pleasant interface is not evidence of belonging or psychological safety.

## Resolve conflicts openly

- **Evidence versus freedom:** Separate supported work from fresh evidence when relevant, but do not test every activity. Play, conversation, tool use, and collaboration need not justify themselves through an individual score.
- **Independence versus access:** Describe the support and narrow the claim when needed. Do not withdraw a necessary accommodation to make a comparison look cleaner. Ambiguous cases need subject and accessibility expertise together.
- **Personalization versus privacy:** Begin with learner- or teacher-chosen options and minimal information. Do not assume a permanent learner profile is necessary. Open source means inspectable materials and code, not public personal data.
- **Ambition versus capacity:** Prefer one maintainable resource to a universal platform. Choose paper, simpler software, revision, or no build when the added technology has no defensible benefit.

## Before expanding beyond the proposal

Prepare one short resource brief, reviewed in proportion to its claims and risks:

1. **Purpose and context:** What is taught, for whom, with which prerequisites and accessible alternatives? Identify the relevant curriculum or standard and any claimed alignment; do not imply official adoption.
2. **Evidence and counterexample:** What supports the choice, what contradicts it, and where does the evidence stop? Describe a fictional failure case and the simpler alternative.
3. **People and costs:** Who authors, checks subject accuracy and developmental fit, reviews access, maintains the material, and handles concerns? Name willing people and relevant competence, not hoped-for staffing.
4. **Decision and revision:** Record the options, unresolved disagreement, chosen direction, reasons, reviewer, and review date. Leave unsupported claims open. A willing specialist's review is not proof of effectiveness.
5. **Pause conditions:** Identify who can pause or withdraw the affected resource if material inaccuracies, exclusion, pressure, privacy exposure, or unsustainable workload emerge. Gains on a narrow task do not override those concerns. Document a correction or a reason not to proceed.

This brief can be drafted now using fictional examples. Any later child participation, field evaluation, or learner-data handling needs a separate plan and accountable authorization, including the applicable safeguarding, consent/assent, privacy, and independent-review requirements. It is not authorized by this document or by finishing the adult proposal's roadmap.
