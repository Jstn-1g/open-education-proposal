# Three ways to help us begin

Pick one task below and comment in its existing discussion. Use the [issue chooser](https://github.com/Jstn-1g/open-education-proposal/issues/new/choose) for a different topic. These tasks are for adults; none involves children, real learner records, or a study. Reading needs no account; posting needs a GitHub account.

You can start with one example, a question about a source, or a barrier you encountered. The criteria below describe a completed piece of work, not a prerequisite for asking for help. Agree a manageable next step with the maintainer; specialist review may require more time or a smaller scope.

## 1. Make the help-versus-access distinction practical

**Best for:** Educators and accessibility practitioners.

**Start small:** [Add one fictional case or question to task #1](https://github.com/Jstn-1g/open-education-proposal/issues/1). You could write: “For a fictional [task], the support [does / does not / might] supply the target skill because […]. I am unsure about […].” Mixed or uncertain cases are welcome; no classification is required to ask for help.

**Deliverable:** A short table of six fictional situations showing the target skill, support used, whether it supplies the skill, and what remains uncertain. Include the same support in two different contexts: reading aloud for a mathematics task and for a word-decoding task.

**Done when:**

- All six situations identify the skill before classifying the support.
- The table distinguishes instructional help from access support and includes at least one case needing specialist judgment.
- A reader can see why a support must not be banned merely because it is assistive.
- Two relevant primary or professional sources are linked, with scope and limitations. Clearly label judgments not established by those sources.

**Starting point:** [Six fictional cases](REVIEW-CASES.md), alongside [the proposal](../PROPOSAL.md) and [source notes](SOURCES.md). The editable case draft is AI-assisted and not yet specialist-reviewed; publishing it does not complete this task. Challenge one case rather than writing a whole new table. Do not create a scoring rule or ready-to-use assessment.

Use the [learning-design questions](LEARNING-DESIGN.md) to challenge one case: could the interpretation withhold access, stigmatize help-seeking, invite unnecessary recording, or add avoidable teacher work? A fictional counterexample belongs within this task, not in an additional required report.

## 2. Check one evidence claim

**Best for:** Researchers, librarians, educators, and careful readers.

**Start small:** [Ask one source question or propose one correction in task #2](https://github.com/Jstn-1g/open-education-proposal/issues/2). You could write: “Does the paper support this sentence: […]? I could not find […] in the source.” Say what you checked, if anything; an unanswered question is useful.

**Deliverable:** A one-page evidence note for the claim that successful assisted work need not establish independent learning. Start with the Bastani study in [source notes](SOURCES.md).

**Done when:**

- The primary paper, authors, publication date, and link are verified.
- The note distinguishes assisted performance from the later outcome and identifies the population, comparison, setting, and study limitations.
- It states what the paper supports and what it does not establish for younger children or this project.
- Any correction to our wording is proposed explicitly; the study is not used to claim that all educational AI helps or harms.

No systematic-review claim is expected from a one-paper check. A second source is useful if it supports a specific correction, not just as another citation.

## 3. Report a barrier to reading or contributing

**Best for:** Accessibility reviewers, designers, writers, and developers.

**Start small:** [Describe one barrier in task #3](https://github.com/Jstn-1g/open-education-proposal/issues/3). You could write: “On [page], I tried to […], but […]. I expected […].” No diagnosis, research, or proposed fix is needed.

**Deliverable:** One accessibility report about the site or contributor documents. A proposed remedy is welcome, not required. You do not need a fix, reproducible example, or proof of disability to report a barrier.

**A useful report describes:**

- The page or document and what you were trying to do.
- What got in the way and what you expected instead.
- Setup or steps you can share, if helpful. No health details are needed.

**Maintainer follow-through:** Record a proposed response, a question, or a reason to defer. Keep unresolved barriers visible. A report is a contribution even when a fix is not yet available.

**If a fix is implemented:** Recheck the affected task in the same conditions where possible, record the result and remaining limits, and distinguish an individual check from whole-site WCAG conformance. Do not call an untested recommendation a verified fix.

Use [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) where relevant. Do not install extra tooling merely to produce a report if a browser or document review is sufficient.

## For every task

Keep examples fictional and submissions free of child, confidential, and private case data. Include sources when making an evidence claim; a question or barrier report needs no citation. Disclose material AI assistance and note uncertainties. See the [contribution guide](../CONTRIBUTING.md) for review and credit.
