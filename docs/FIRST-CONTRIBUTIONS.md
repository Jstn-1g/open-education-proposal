# Three ways to help us begin

Pick one small deliverable and use the [issue chooser](https://github.com/Jstn-1g/open-education-proposal/issues/new/choose) to discuss it. These tasks are for adults; none involves children, real learner records, or a study.

## 1. Make the help-versus-access distinction practical

**Best for:** Educators and accessibility practitioners.

**Deliverable:** A short table of six fictional situations showing the target skill, support used, whether it supplies the skill, and what remains uncertain. Include the same support in two different contexts: reading aloud for a mathematics task and for a word-decoding task.

**Done when:**

- All six situations identify the skill before classifying the support.
- The table distinguishes instructional help from access support and includes at least one case needing specialist judgment.
- A reader can see why a support must not be banned merely because it is assistive.
- Two relevant primary or professional sources are linked, with scope and limitations. Clearly label judgments not established by those sources.

**Starting point:** [The proposal](../PROPOSAL.md) and [source notes](SOURCES.md). Do not create a scoring rule or ready-to-use assessment.

## 2. Check one evidence claim

**Best for:** Researchers, librarians, educators, and careful readers.

**Deliverable:** A one-page evidence note for the claim that successful assisted work need not establish independent learning. Start with the Bastani study in [source notes](SOURCES.md).

**Done when:**

- The primary paper, authors, publication date, and link are verified.
- The note distinguishes assisted performance from the later outcome and identifies the population, comparison, setting, and study limitations.
- It states what the paper supports and what it does not establish for younger children or this project.
- Any correction to our wording is proposed explicitly; the study is not used to claim that all educational AI helps or harms.

No systematic-review claim is expected from a one-paper check. A second source is useful if it supports a specific correction, not just as another citation.

## 3. Remove one barrier to reading or contributing

**Best for:** Accessibility reviewers, designers, writers, and developers.

**Deliverable:** One documented accessibility finding on the site or contributor documents, plus a focused change or recommendation. Reproduction details help, but are not a condition for reporting a barrier.

**Done when:**

- The report identifies the page, setup, steps, expected behavior, and observed barrier.
- It covers one concrete need, such as heading navigation, keyboard focus, zoom and reflow, contrast, or understandable instructions.
- The change is checked in the same conditions and the result is recorded.
- Any automated result is described as a check, not proof that the whole site conforms to WCAG.

Use [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) where relevant. Do not install extra tooling merely to produce a report if a browser or document review is sufficient.

## For every task

Keep examples fictional and submissions free of child, confidential, and private case data. Name your sources, disclose material AI assistance, and note uncertainties. See the [contribution guide](../CONTRIBUTING.md) for review and credit.
