How to read the moving lines

Each line shows one pendulum’s angle, from −8° to +8°. Both use the same four-second window.

When the lines match, the rhythms match in this model. This does not mean the balls move at the same speed.

Why does the model do that?

It uses T = 2π√(L/g), with g = 9.81 m/s². Mass is absent from this formula. Both start at 8°. We approximate small swings and ignore air resistance, friction, string mass, and stretch.

A model shows consequences of its assumptions. It cannot, by itself, establish what every real pendulum does.

What would you keep the same in a real comparison? What might this model leave out?

For educators & reviewers · Purpose, evidence, and limits

Age 8: a quantity stays the same

Proposed goal: connect equal partitions to equivalent fractions of a fixed whole. Assumes familiarity with equal shares; age alone does not establish readiness.

The IES fractions guide recommends number lines and understanding fractions as numbers. Its relevant recommendation has moderate evidence. Our connection from pieces to symbols to a number line is a design application—not a tested intervention.

IES fractions guidance · IES guidance on representations and worked examples

A randomized fraction-game study found no transfer to several untrained fraction tasks. Enjoyment and successful matching are not proof of understanding. Nuraydin and colleagues, 2022.

Paper alternative: fold two identical strips to show one half and two quarters. Discuss what changed and what stayed the same.

Age 14: investigate one factor

Proposed goal: distinguish a controlled comparison from changing two factors, and distinguish a model's prediction from real-world evidence. Assumes an understanding of length, mass, and elapsed time; no algebra is required to interact.

The physics follows the ideal small-angle pendulum model in OpenStax University Physics, §15.4. The curriculum connection is to controlling variables and examining models in NGSS Appendix F; this is not a claim of certified alignment or effectiveness.

Showing a prediction, visual comparison, and short explanation is an evidence-informed design choice. It does not demonstrate that this prototype teaches scientific reasoning.

Paper alternative: compare supplied setup diagrams and calculated periods, identifying which variables differ. No physical experiment is requested here.
