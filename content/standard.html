The draft proposal

Help is useful.
Understanding needs evidence.

When claiming independent understanding, distinguish instructional help from evidence on fresh, valid work. Keep necessary access support. Not every activity needs a test or record.

01 / A practical change

Make the difference
part of the record.

We propose an evidence-reporting practice for education using technology. An assisted response and a later demonstration answer different questions; combining them into a single “learning” score can hide that difference.

It applies to AI explanations, worked examples, human hints, and other instructional help. It does not assume all existing assessment ignores this distinction, or propose replacing every curriculum standard.

Assisted performance

“An example helped me solve it.”

A fictional learner compares 3/8 and 3/5 after seeing a diagram and a worked example. The description identifies that help rather than claiming the response alone proves understanding.

What it tells us: what was possible with that instruction.

Later learner evidence

“Here is why this comparison works.”

Later, the learner explains a fresh same-numerator fraction comparison. Keep necessary access support. If it also supplies part of the skill, describe supported performance and narrow the claim.

What it could tell us: what this task supports, if the task and timing are valid.

Illustration only, not a validated assessment or an instruction to test children. Never remove necessary support to manufacture an independent result. One example does not establish retention, transfer, or effectiveness.

02 / Four rules to scrutinize

Challenge the proposal.
Make it more useful.

1. Describe help relevant to the claim.

An assisted response alone cannot establish independent performance. This is not a requirement to log every hint or test every activity.

2. Preserve access support.

A screen reader, accessible format, communication aid, or other support may be essential. Keep it available; narrow the claim or leave it unresolved when needed. Mixed cases require subject and accessibility judgment.

3. Be precise about the evidence.

A claim about retention needs valid delayed work. A claim about transfer needs a meaningfully changed task and evidence of validity. Engagement, speed, and assisted accuracy are not substitutes.

4. Let evidence determine the method.

Compare a proposed tool with credible simpler approaches. Examine benefits, harms, access, and burden. Prefer the simplest method that meets the need; that may involve no AI or no software.

What still needs work: definitions of “meaningful help,” appropriate delays, task validity, subject-specific examples, teacher workload, and accessibility review. These are questions for contributors, not settled results.
