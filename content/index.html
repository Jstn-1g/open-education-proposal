02 / The wider proposal

What should a learner
be able to do next?

Technology can change how we teach, practice, and assess. When a resource claims independent understanding, our proposal asks it to distinguish instructional help from the evidence for that claim.

Read the proposal →
01

Make help visible.

Describe which explanation, prompt, example, or tool supported a response. Keep the work’s context with the work.

02

Check what remains.

Look for understanding on fresh work after a delay. A changed task needs its own evidence that it measures the intended skill.

03

Keep people in charge.

Preserve accessibility, human judgment, and the freedom to choose a simpler method. No AI is a valid outcome.

More capable people.
More room to be human.

Curiosity, imagination, relationships, and judgment belong at the center. The proposal excludes robot tutors, AI companions, and simulated relationships with children.

03 / Build a public commons

A big mission.
A useful first step.

The ambition is an open learning path that educators and families can adapt, with no required proprietary model. We begin with a small proposal people can challenge and improve.

  1. Now: examine the proposal. This edition offers evidence notes, website code, and a contributor kit for adults—not a curriculum, an adopted standard, or a proven intervention.
  2. Next: improve three small pieces. The six-case discussion draft, one evidence note, and one accessibility report with a maintainer response. These are first-month targets, not promised deadlines; the case draft still needs qualified review.
  3. Later: consider a small learning resource. Only with a separate plan, qualified human review, maintenance capacity, and evidence appropriate to its claims.

Code uses Apache-2.0; original content uses CC BY 4.0. Human teaching, relationships, and judgment stay central. AI is optional.
