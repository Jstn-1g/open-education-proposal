The change we propose

01 / During learning

What help made possible.

A worked example, a teacher’s hint, or an AI explanation can help someone produce an answer.

Record the instructional help.
02 / After learning

What understanding remains.

Use fresh, valid work later to examine what the learner can explain and apply.

Keep access support available.

Report these separately. A polished answer alone cannot establish learning.

01 / Start with a better question

What should a learner
be able to do next?

Technology can change how we teach, practice, and assess. Our proposal asks education systems to make instructional assistance visible and distinguish it from evidence of lasting understanding.

01

Make help visible.

Describe which explanation, prompt, example, or tool supported a response. Keep the work’s context with the work.

02

Check what remains.

Look for understanding on fresh work after a delay. A changed task needs its own evidence that it measures the intended skill.

03

Keep people in charge.

Preserve accessibility, human judgment, and the freedom to choose a simpler method. No AI is a valid outcome.

More capable people.
More room to be human.

Curiosity, imagination, relationships, and judgment belong at the center. The proposal excludes robot tutors, AI companions, and simulated relationships with children.

02 / Build a public commons

Useful work.
Open to improvement.

We intend to release the proposal, website code, and review materials under open licenses. The longer-term ambition is learning materials and tools that people can understand, adapt, and use without a proprietary model.

This first edition is a proposal for adult collaboration. It is not a classroom product, an adopted standard, or a proven intervention. License approval and repository setup are still pending.
